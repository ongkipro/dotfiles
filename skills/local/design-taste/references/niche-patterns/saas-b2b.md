# SaaS and B2B

Structure: [README.md](README.md). Software, tools, and services sold to teams.
A starting hypothesis; inspected references and the owner's brand overrule it.

## 1. Job and decision path

Several people decide: a user who feels the problem, a buyer who approves cost,
sometimes IT/security. Entry from search, a referral, or content → "does it
solve my specific workflow, does it fit our stack, what does it cost, is it
safe?" → trial, demo, or contact sales → onboarding. Pages must serve a
skimming evaluator and a detailed checker.

## 2. Market conventions — VERIFY per project

- Pricing in IDR/MYR or USD, with or without tax (PPN/SST); monthly vs annual;
  whether prices are published at all.
- Local invoicing, bank transfer or local payment gateways, and a WhatsApp
  sales contact are common expectations for SMB buyers in the region.
- Data residency or local-law questions from buyers; publish only what the
  owner can substantiate.
- Bahasa interface and support availability.

## 3. Must-have content inventory

- The specific job the product does, shown with **real product screenshots** or
  a short real recording (not drawn in divs).
- Feature detail by workflow, integrations that actually exist, plan limits.
- Pricing or the honest path to a quote; trial terms.
- Security/privacy facts that are true, documentation and support channels.
- Customer logos, case studies, and numbers only with permission and source.

## 5. Recurring patterns

| Problem | Pattern | Does not apply when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What does it actually do?" | Workflow sections each pairing one job with a real screenshot | pre-launch: label concept screens as concept |
| Plan comparison | Equal plan columns with aligned rows and a clear recommended plan only if the owner chooses one | single price |
| Different readers | Short scannable summary, with depth in docs and security pages | — |
| Proof | Named customer stories with permission; integrations list that links to docs | no customers yet: say what exists (beta, pilot) |
| One intent, one label | A single primary label for trial/demo across the site (tells-scan CTA-intent check) | — |

## 6. Category default to escape

Centered hero with gradient blob, a fake dashboard drawn in divs, "AI-powered"
and "seamless" headings, a logo strip of unknown companies, a bento grid of
icon features, "Get started" / "Start free trial" / "Sign up" all on one page.
Familiar shapes are fine when the content fills them honestly.

## 7. Optional style directions

| Direction | Fit | Risk | A11y cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product-first (screenshots lead) | mature product with good UI | exposes UI weakness | screenshots need text alternatives |
| Editorial/problem-led | new category, education needed | slow to reach product | long pages need navigation |
| Dense technical (docs-like) | developer tools, IT buyers | intimidating for SMB owners | low |

## 8. Mobile notes

Evaluators often read on mobile and buy on desktop: pricing tables reflow into
per-plan blocks; screenshots are cropped to the relevant region, not shrunk;
demo/contact form short; WhatsApp sales contact if the owner uses it.

## 9. Claim traps

Uptime or performance percentages without monitoring evidence, "bank-grade
security", compliance badges not actually held, invented ROI or time-saved
numbers, customer logos without permission, "AI" claims for features that are
rule-based.

## 10. Open questions

Who signs off the purchase? Published pricing and currency? Trial or demo?
Real screenshots and customer permissions? Security/compliance facts to state?
