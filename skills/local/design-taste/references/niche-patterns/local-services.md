# Local Services

Structure: [README.md](README.md). Clinics, salons, workshops, contractors,
tutors, consultants, and similar location- or area-bound services. A starting
hypothesis; inspected references and the owner's brand overrule it.

## 1. Job and decision path

Entry from maps, local search, a referral, or social → "do they do my exact
service, in my area, at a price I accept, and can I trust them?" → contact
(WhatsApp, call, booking form) → visit or appointment → review. Most decisions
end in a conversation, not a checkout.

## 2. Market conventions — VERIFY per project

- WhatsApp is often the primary action; confirm number, hours of response, and
  pre-filled message.
- Price lists may be "starting from" or on request; confirm what the owner will
  publish.
- Licences or professional registration relevant to the trade (health,
  education, construction) — confirm which apply and how they may be displayed.
- Google Business Profile reviews are common proof; link to the source rather
  than copying selected quotes without context.
- Service-area names in Bahasa as people search for them.

## 3. Must-have content inventory

- Service list with what is included, duration, and price or price basis.
- Service area, address, hours, map, contact channels.
- Real photos of the place, team, and work (with consent); licences or
  certificates that actually exist.
- Process: what happens after contact, how booking works, cancellation rules.

## 4. Reference set (to research)

- "<service> <city> website" for 2-3 local competitors (e.g. "klinik gigi
  Jakarta website", "bengkel mobil Bandung website")
- "<service> booking website" (directional, may be global)
- "local service business website service area page"
- Google Maps listings of competitors to see what proof customers already use

## 5. Recurring patterns

| Problem | Pattern | Does not apply when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Do you do this?" | Service list as scannable rows with plain names people search for | single-service business |
| "Can I trust you?" | Real team/place photos, licence numbers, linked reviews, years in business only if true | — |
| "How much?" | Price or price basis per service; what changes the price | owner declines to publish: say "on request" honestly |
| Contact friction | One primary contact action repeated after decision points, with hours and response expectation | — |
| "Is this near me?" | Service area named and mapped | online-only service |

## 6. Category default to escape

Stock handshake or smiling-team photos, a hero slider, "trusted professionals"
and "quality service" headings, an icon grid of six generic services, an
invented "500+ happy clients" counter. Fine only when chosen with real content.

## 7. Optional style directions

| Direction | Fit | Risk | A11y cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Calm clinical (health, education) | trust-heavy services | sterile, interchangeable | low |
| Craft/workshop (trades, makers) | portfolio of real work | heavy imagery, slow pages | alt text for work photos |
| Friendly neighbourhood | salons, tutors, small shops | over-casual for regulated trades | illustration must not replace facts |

## 8. Mobile notes

Tap-to-WhatsApp and tap-to-call within reach on every service view; address
and hours copyable as text; booking form short with correct input types; maps
link opens the native app.

## 9. Claim traps

Guaranteed outcomes (medical, academic, legal), "No. 1 in <city>", certificates
or awards without documents, review counts or ratings typed in by hand,
before/after images without consent.

## 10. Open questions

Primary contact channel and response hours? Published prices? Licences to
display? Service area? Real photos and review source?
