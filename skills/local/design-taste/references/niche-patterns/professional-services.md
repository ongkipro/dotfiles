# Professional Services (B2B)

Structure: [README.md](README.md). Firms that sell expertise or capacity to
other businesses: sales outsourcing and telemarketing, consulting, marketing
or creative agencies, accounting and tax, legal, IT services, recruitment,
corporate training. A starting hypothesis; inspected references and the
owner's brand overrule it.

## 1. Job and decision path

A manager with a concrete problem (pipeline too thin, no in-house team, a
compliance deadline) arrives from search, a referral, LinkedIn, or a tender
list → "do they understand my problem, have they done it for a business like
mine, who will actually do the work, how do they engage and charge, can I
trust them?" → contact form, consultation booking, or WhatsApp → proposal or
pitch → contract. The site is often checked a second time by a director or
procurement before a meeting, so facts must be findable in seconds.

## 2. Market conventions — VERIFY per project

- WhatsApp or phone as the real contact channel versus a form; whether the
  owner wants a consultation booking at all.
- Company legality shown to B2B buyers (in Indonesia: legal entity type,
  NIB, NPWP for invoicing; licensed professions such as accounting, tax, and
  law have their own registration) — show only what the owner confirms.
- Pricing: rarely published; engagement models (retainer, project, per-seat,
  success fee) are more useful than a price table.
- Language: Bahasa for local SMB buyers, English or bilingual for corporate
  or regional clients.
- Client names and logos usually require written permission; many contracts
  forbid naming the client.

## 3. Must-have content inventory

- The specific problems solved, in the client's words, per service line.
- How an engagement works: steps from first call to delivery, typical
  duration, what the client must provide.
- Engagement and pricing model, or the honest path to a quote.
- The people: named leads with real photos, roles, and relevant experience.
- Proof the owner can substantiate: case studies with permission, measured
  outcomes with their basis, certifications actually held.
- Industries or company sizes served, and what the firm does not take on.
- Contact details, office location if clients visit, response-time promise
  only if the owner keeps it.

## 4. Reference set (to research)

- "jasa <service> B2B" / "<service> outsourcing Indonesia" for local
  competitors (e.g. "jasa outsourcing sales", "jasa telemarketing B2B",
  "konsultan pajak perusahaan")
- "<service> agency website" and "consulting firm case study page"
  (directional, global)
- the sites of 1-2 respected firms in an adjacent profession for tone and
  proof presentation
- Nicelydone / SaaS Landing Page are poor fits here: they show software, not
  services; prefer live firm sites

## 5. Recurring patterns

| Problem | Pattern | Does not apply when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Do they get my problem?" | Service sections that open with the client's situation and the outcome, then the method | a single, self-explanatory service |
| "Who does the work?" | Team section with named people, real photos, and the role each plays in an engagement | firm policy forbids staff names: describe roles and seniority instead |
| "How does it work?" | A short engagement sequence (the real steps, numbered because they are a sequence) | ad-hoc work with no fixed process |
| "Can I trust them?" | One or two deep case studies (context → approach → result with its basis) instead of a wall of logos | no permitted cases yet: show method, credentials, and scope honestly |
| Second reader checking facts | Compact facts block: entity, years active, coverage, engagement models, contact | — |
| One intent, one label | A single primary label for the enquiry action across the site | — |

## 6. Category default to escape

Corporate stock photos of handshakes and people pointing at screens, "your
trusted partner" / "solusi terbaik" / "berpengalaman dan profesional"
headings, a counter strip of unverifiable numbers (clients, projects, years),
an icon grid of six vague services, a logo wall of unknown or unpermitted
clients, a testimonial carousel without names. Familiar shapes are fine when
the content fills them honestly.

## 7. Optional style directions

| Direction | Fit | Risk | A11y cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial expert (type-led, long-form insight) | consulting, legal, advisory | slow for buyers who want contact fast | long pages need clear navigation and a short summary |
| Results-led operational (outcomes, process, numbers with basis) | sales outsourcing, telemarketing, IT services | invites invented metrics | figures need text, not only charts |
| People-first (team and culture lead) | agencies, training, recruitment | weak when the team is small or changing | photos need real alt text |

## 8. Mobile notes

First contact often happens on a phone from a WhatsApp or LinkedIn link:
the service summary and the contact action must be reachable without long
scrolling, the facts block reads as a short list, case studies keep the
result line near the top, and forms ask only what the first call needs.

## 9. Claim traps

"Number 1" or "terbaik" without a source, client counts and conversion or
revenue uplift without a basis, client logos or names without permission,
certifications or memberships not actually held, guaranteed results
("pasti closing", "dijamin naik"), regulated-profession titles used without
the licence.

## 10. Open questions

Which service lines matter most, and which are being phased out? Who is the
buyer (owner, sales head, procurement)? Engagement models and whether prices
or ranges may be shown? Which clients and results may be named? Who on the
team can appear with name and photo? Preferred contact channel and expected
response time?
