# Human Writing

## Diagnose before rewriting

Evaluate patterns by severity, frequency, and context. A single instance is rarely a problem.

### High-impact problems

- Empty openings that delay the point.
- Abstract claims without actors, actions, or consequences.
- Repeated sentence templates or paragraph rhythms.
- Mechanical transitions such as repeated "Moreover," "Furthermore," or "In conclusion."
- Unnecessary restatement of the same claim.
- Inflated wording that makes an ordinary point sound momentous.
- Generic endings that summarize without advancing or closing the task.

### Medium-impact problems

- Excessive symmetry, stacked triads, or repeated contrast formulas.
- Too many fragments or uniformly short sentences.
- Vague intensifiers such as very, highly, truly, and significantly.
- Excessive nominalization where a direct verb is clearer.
- Unmotivated shifts between formal and conversational registers.

### Second-order structural & rhythmic anti-slop (structural AI tells)

- **Three-clause sentence rhythm:** Metronomic cadence of repeating `[clause 1], [clause 2], [clause 3]` patterns across consecutive sentences.
- **Question-cadence H2s:** Forcing every section heading into a rhetorical question rather than natural declarative or noun phrases.
- **Hedge stacking:** Clustered uncertainty words (*"may, typically, often, generally, perhaps, somewhat"*) within 20 words that obscure confidence without adding precision.
- **False-balance framing:** Unmotivated *"While X, also Y"* or *"On one hand X, on the other Y"* when no genuine contradiction or substantive contrast exists.
- **Symmetric list bloat:** Forcing every bullet point in a list to exactly the same length (e.g. ~80 words each) regardless of the actual density of evidence.
- **Formulaic openers:** Monotonous paragraph lead-ins like *"Here's why"*, *"At its core"*, or *"To understand X, we must first look at Y"*.

### Low-impact signals

Do not flag these alone: passive voice, adverbs, em dashes, rhetorical questions, sentence openers with "And" or "But," or a three-item list.

## Rewrite method

1. State the point earlier.
2. Replace abstraction with an actor, action, object, or consequence when supported.
3. Cut duplicated meaning before changing vocabulary.
4. Break metronomic 3-clause rhythms and vary sentence lengths based on idea complexity.
5. Strip hedge stacking; state supported facts directly and cite their limits cleanly.
6. Use transitions that reveal the true relationship between ideas.
7. Keep distinctive phrases, humor, cultural texture, and deliberate roughness when they serve the voice.
8. End with the required conclusion, decision, instruction, or CTA.

Do not introduce slang, typos, false anecdotes, fake hesitation, irrelevant personal detail, or artificial inconsistency merely to appear human.
