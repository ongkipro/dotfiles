# Human Writing

## Diagnose before rewriting

Evaluate patterns by severity, frequency, and context. A single instance is rarely a problem.

### High-impact problems

- Empty openings that delay the point.
- Abstract claims without actors, actions, or consequences.
- Repeated sentence templates or paragraph rhythms.
- Mechanical transitions such as repeated "Moreover," "Furthermore," or "In conclusion."
- Unnecessary restatement of the same claim.
- Inflated wording that makes an ordinary point sound momentous.
- Generic endings that summarize without advancing or closing the task.

### Medium-impact problems

- Excessive symmetry, stacked triads, or repeated contrast formulas.
- Too many fragments or uniformly short sentences.
- Vague intensifiers such as very, highly, truly, and significantly.
- Excessive nominalization where a direct verb is clearer.
- Unmotivated shifts between formal and conversational registers.

### Low-impact signals

Do not flag these alone: passive voice, adverbs, em dashes, rhetorical questions, sentence openers with "And" or "But," or a three-item list.

## Rewrite method

1. State the point earlier.
2. Replace abstraction with an actor, action, object, or consequence when supported.
3. Cut duplicated meaning before changing vocabulary.
4. Vary sentence structure because the ideas require it, not to simulate randomness.
5. Use transitions that reveal the relationship between ideas.
6. Keep distinctive phrases, humor, cultural texture, and deliberate roughness when they serve the voice.
7. End with the required conclusion, decision, instruction, or CTA.

Do not introduce slang, typos, false anecdotes, fake hesitation, irrelevant personal detail, or artificial inconsistency merely to appear human.
