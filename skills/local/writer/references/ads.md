# Ads Writing

Align hook, promise, proof, and CTA. Keep each ad focused on one dominant angle.

## Meta and social ads

- Earn attention quickly without misleading clickbait.
- Match the creative and landing-page promise.
- Use natural, scannable line breaks.
- Avoid unsupported personal-attribute assumptions and prohibited or sensitive claims.
- House rules for Meta ad fields (soft CTA, no link/price, Flexible Format/DCO
  combinations) live in `copywriting/references/meta-ads.md`; this file owns
  preservation and localization only.
- Write each market natively: Indonesian (`id-ID`) and Malaysian Malay
  (`ms-MY`) are separate deliverables, never word-swapped from one another
  (see `two-way-localization.md`). Keep every qualifier in the same field as
  its claim, because Meta can drop or recombine fields.

ID vs MY vocabulary traps (examples, not a full glossary; mark native review
pending when unsure):

| Meaning | `id-ID` | `ms-MY` | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| free (of charge) | gratis | percuma | `percuma` in Indonesian means "useless" |
| can | bisa | boleh | `boleh` in Indonesian means "may/allowed" |
| need | butuh, perlu | perlu | `butuh` is vulgar slang in MY: never use there |
| size | ukuran | saiz | — |
| bag | tas | beg | — |
| car | mobil | kereta | `kereta` in Indonesian means "train" |
| broken | rusak | rosak | — |
| easy / happy | mudah / senang | senang (easy) / gembira | `senang` means "happy" in ID, "easy" in MY |
| you (ad register) | kamu / Anda | anda / korang (casual) | pick per brand voice, keep consistent |

## Search ads

- Reflect query intent and landing-page content.
- Preserve supplied character limits.
- Use concrete differentiators and clear action language.
- Do not repeat near-identical headlines merely to fill slots.

Check claim support, offer consistency, CTA clarity, destination match, and platform constraints supplied by the user.
